The pinned GEMSDOE32 README describes the champion as a dotted potential-field family with catalogue-flank pruning, not an independently discovered neural geothermal detector. It reports removal of 2,545 dots from a 40,199-dot parent, leaving 37,654. This review did not download the parent/child rasters and does not claim pixel-exact reproduction. Some peer prose calls the whole history nested, while GEMSDOE25 warns of non-nested thinning; resolve this from raster bytes before repeating the entire nesting claim.

The mechanism is mathematically plausible: with weighted true-positive credit T, false-positive mass F and withheld-positive count G, the implemented metric is

`DTI = T / (0.2*T + 0.2*F + 0.8*G)`.

Removing redundant or uncredited mass reduces the denominator with little recall loss. A 300 m triangular kernel gives proximity credit, so isolated dots can retain much of a continuous line's coverage while spending less false-positive mass. However, pruning near the **full** catalogue before withholding segments is a distribution shift/leakage hazard for hide-and-recover. Recompute every catalogue-dependent operation from visible faults within each fold, and mask visible faults pixel-exactly. A gain from pruning is not proof of geothermal vents, normal-fault dip, or physical neural training. GEMS predicts faults; geothermal relevance is a separate scientific interpretation.

**Can we exceed the reported champion?** Possible, but not established here. Regional coverage, calibration and sparse placement can all help or hurt. The only defensible next claim is a paired gain under the same frozen independent holdout, with uncertainty, followed by an independent selector decision. No projected leaderboard score is provided.
