## Selected lead and falsification plan

H1 is the first candidate because it can start with the competition's own feature stack and it explicitly uses the user's required regional stress direction as a *differentiable constraint*, while retaining enough flexibility to falsify that prior. H2 is a lower-cost comparator; H3 is a separate physical-method ablation. Do not select a leaderboard submission from this ranking.

Predeclare the comparison before scoring:

1. Reproduce the organizer reference on the authorized local rasters.
2. Create contiguous spatial tile folds (default 512-pixel tiles), with at least a 30-pixel collar removed from training-label masks and the published metric's 3-pixel evaluation buffer. Where vector IDs permit, hold out whole fault systems rather than fragments.
3. Compare baseline vs. H1 with the same labels, folds, candidate emission budget, and exact DTI implementation. Report pooled and per-fold scores, not random-pixel CV.
4. Ablate the stress term (`lambda=0`), the stress-angle field (spatially shuffled), and the geophysical-lineament family. A benefit that survives only a particular fold or an unshuffled prior is not robust evidence.
5. Promote only if the predeclared pooled DTI improves and at least 4/5 folds improve; report all fold values and a proxy-generalization caveat. Do not spend a weekly submission on a failed or unrun test.

## Stress constraint: required geometry, no universal compass filter

The official problem description lists dilatation rate, shear strain rate, and the second invariant among features. These are scalar summaries; they do **not** uniquely determine the local principal-axis orientation. A full horizontal strain tensor, or a defensible focal-mechanism stress inversion, is needed. Nevada Geodetic Laboratory's MAGNET products provide station locations and ENU velocity estimates; turning sparse velocities into a local strain field is a modeling step, not a direct observation at every 100 m pixel.

For a normal-faulting regime, the plan-view normal to a trace is expected to be broadly compatible with the horizontal extension axis. A differentiable structure-tensor term is implemented in `src/gemsdoe36/orientation.py`:

\[
L = L_{base} + \lambda_s L_{axial}, \qquad
L_{axial}=\frac{\sum_x c_x q_x e_x\,[1-\cos(2(\theta_{\nabla p,x}-\theta_{ext,x}))]}{\sum_x c_xq_xe_x+\epsilon}.
\]

Here \(\theta_{\nabla p}\) is the local predicted-trace normal; \(\theta_{ext}\) is a locally estimated extension axis; \(c_x\) is stress-field confidence; \(q_x\) is image-structure coherence; and \(e_x\) is local structure-tensor energy. The factor of 2 makes directions axial (modulo 180°). Flat outputs receive no orientation gradient, so the term regularizes candidate lines trained by the base objective—it does not hallucinate faults by itself. Mixed normal/strike-slip regimes, anisotropy, uncertainty, and raster-axis sign convention must be handled explicitly. The function is unit-tested for differentiability; it has **not** been trained or tuned on competition data.
