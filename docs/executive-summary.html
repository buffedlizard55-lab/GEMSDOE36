Metric Autopsy

Why GEMSDOE32 Reached 0.2778

The competition DTI metric evaluates distance-weighted overlap with asymmetric weights (α = 0.2, β = 0.8) and triangular decay over 300 m:

DTI = TP_w / [0.2·|P| + 0.8·|G| + 0.8·(TP_g - TP_p)]

Because the competition evaluation engine masks existing USGS and INGENIOUS catalogue faults, predicted dots on or within 200 m of known faults earn 0 true positive credit, yet each dot adds +0.2 to the denominator. GEMSDOE32 applied a catalogue-flank exclusion buffer (B = 2 pixels / 200 m), eliminating 2,545 dots that fell near catalogue traces. This reduced the denominator penalty by 509 units and lifted the score from 0.2708 to 0.2778.

Kinematic Analysis

Why 0.2778 Plateaued & How We Reach 0.3195

Our strike orientation audit revealed that 26.5% (9,972 dots) of GEMSDOE32's predictions struck E-W (070°–115°), directly parallel to the regional extension axis σ3 ∼ 105°. Under Anderson's (1905) theory of faulting, normal faulting parallel to σ3 is mechanically impossible. These dots are false positive noise (flight-line artifacts, contacts, drainage).

GEMSDOE36 targets the top score of 0.3195 by using Raissi et al. (2019) physics-informed loss to heavily penalize E-W strikes ($113\times$ higher loss) and reallocating dot budget to 465 high-temperature ($T \ge 130^\circ\text{C}$) blind geothermal conduits from GDR 1391.
